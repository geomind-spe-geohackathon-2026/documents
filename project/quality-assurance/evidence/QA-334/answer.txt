So roughly 24 MW of the peak is reservoir heat and 3.6 MW is electricity; the boiler trims the last 2.6 MW. The heat pump's sink rises from 262.2265 MW to 277.2265 MW, i.e. it runs at its full 15.0 MW.

Is ~35 K brine cooling consistent with 2 × 300 m3/h? Yes. At that step the brine flow is 599.9955 m3/h (171.665 kg/s) and the brine cools from 85.028 degC at the wellhead to 52.196 degC after the heat pump — 32.83 K (calculation C52cde1). The year's worst is 35.52 K (d0, 2027-07-19) and 34.77 K (d1, 2027-05-25), against the case's 40 K limit: both pass. The worst cooling is in summer, not January, because the chiller then draws heat from the same 100 degC loop.

Why an absorption chiller, not a compression chiller: two reasons.

gemini has no compression chiller module. Its cooling category holds only AbsorptionUnit and AdsorptionChiller (list_modules), so a compression chiller could not be modelled here at all — this is a limit of the tool, not a finding about the site.
The design has 100 degC water to spare. The absorption chiller (10.0 MW, thermal COP 0.7) is driven by that water and needs almost no power of its own: 237.425 MWh/yr of electricity for 23743 MWh of cold, plus 1157.96 MWh/yr for its dry cooler. On a plant that already buys 26728 MWh/yr of electricity, making cold from heat rather than from power is the cheaper route — and it is why geothermal cold share is 100 %.

Caveat: the 24 / 3.6 / 2.6 MW split is a single day's snapshot from the daily-step run, not a design guarantee; the brine cooling figure is the one the case's requirement judges, and it passes at 35.52 K against 40 K.
