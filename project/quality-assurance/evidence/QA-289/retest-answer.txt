Two plant versions, side by side — recommendation: build version 1

So this is a real comparison of two designs, not of two results of one design.

Checks against the challenge limits — both pass. Both deliver the full demand (heating and cooling coverage 1.0, unmet heat and cold 0), both peak at 30.0 MW heat and 10.0 MW cold, exactly the challenge's 30.0 MWth and 10.0 MWcool, and both ran with 0 failed steps and gemini's verdict "feasible: true, reasons: none". For v1 gemini passed all 15 requirement checks; for v2 gemini's overall verdict is passed, and I have its peak, coverage and failure figures but not the item-by-item check list. The challenge PDF states no LCOH, LCOC or CO2 limit, so those three are reported, not judged.

Which one I would build: version 1. It needs 10.7 MEUR more up front, but it is cheaper to run (3.654 vs 4.978 MEUR/yr), cheaper per MWh of heat, cold and thermal supply, and emits about a third less CO2 (13.77 vs 20.96 kt). The reason is visible in the energy figures: v1's heat pump carries the heat and the boiler only trims the peak (boiler heat 253.9 MWh, no gas bought), while v2 burns gas for most of the heat (boiler heat 76829.96 MWh, gas bought 65189.53 MWh). Version 2 is the right choice only if the capital budget is the binding constraint.

What could improve it

improvement: version 1 — shrink the 30.0 MW boiler, which peaks at 2.636 MW (8.787 % of capacity): it carries CAPEX and fixed OPEX for capacity that is never used.
improvement: version 2 — add a heat pump to move heat off the gas boiler: its fuel OPEX is 2.604 MEUR/yr and its CO2 20.96 kt, the two figures that make it lose to version 1.

Caveats: no uncertainty or sensitivity study was run on either design, so all figures are single-point values on the case's prices; LCOH and LCOC rest on the case's 80/20 heat/cold cost allocation, LCTS does not.
