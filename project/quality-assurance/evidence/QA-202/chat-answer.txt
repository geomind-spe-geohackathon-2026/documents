Is the CO2 of the grid electricity priced too? No. In this case's economics the carbon price applies only to the plant's own CO2 and methane — on-site combustion (gas boiler, CHP, flare) and the CO2 and methane released from the brine. The CO2 of purchased grid electricity is counted in the CO2 metric but is not priced again, because the electricity price already carries the generators' ETS cost; pricing it a second time in the plant's opex would count it twice.

The case paths behind that:

economics.carbon.price = 51.55865788847466 EUR/t (row carbon-price-eu-ets-esma-2025) — the only carbon price in the case.
economics.energy.electricity = 105.75 EUR/MWh — the grid electricity price, which already embeds the generators' carbon cost.
utilities.electricity.co2_intensity = 300 g/kWh (organisers' reference row co2-grid-organisers-2026) — a case value used for the CO2 metric, not an economics key, so it is not priced.

Sources: knowledge/handbook-economics.md section 3.6 ("Carbon cost in the opex": grid electricity's CO2 is already in the electricity price where the generators pay for allowances) and section 16.7 ("Conventions and limits": the carbon price applies to the plant's own CO2 and methane only; grid electricity's CO2 is counted in the co2 metric but not priced). Section 8.2 notes that a social view may apply a shadow price to all emissions including grid electricity, but then must not also count the carbon cost inside the electricity price — this case uses the financial convention.

If you would rather price grid electricity's CO2 as well (a social/analyst view), that is a change of the case's convention and I would need your go-ahead before it is applied.
