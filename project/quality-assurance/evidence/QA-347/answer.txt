Why design/v1 uses an absorption chiller

The chiller in design/v1 is an AbsorptionUnit (mode chiller, capacity 10 MW, thermal_cop 0.7, minimum_driving_temperature 80 degC). Its generator is fed from loads.branch2_out — the 100 degC hot-water line that the heat pump and the peak boiler already produce for the heating grid — and it rejects its heat to the existing chiller_cooler dry cooler (design/v1, read_plant; connections loads.branch2_out → chiller.generator_in, chiller.cooling_out → chiller_cooler.fluid_in).

So the design reason is a heat-driven one: the plant already has a 100 degC supply line and a 40 MW dry cooler, and the absorption chiller turns that heat into cold with almost no electricity of its own — 237.42528 MWh over the whole year (result/b028bd65-8c35-4e44-ae84-2ad067938ccc, read_result path chiller). The case's cooling duty is a low-lift one (supply 20 degC, return 25 degC, case demand.cooling), which is exactly the regime a single-effect LiBr unit serves. The alternative, an electric compression chiller, would move the cooling load onto the grid instead: the case's grid carries 300 g/kWh CO2 (case utilities.electricity.co2_intensity), and bought electricity is already the largest CO2 block of this plant at 8.0184 kt of the 13.774 kt total.

Can the compression option be simulated for comparison?

Not in this chat, and I could not produce the comparison numbers. gemini does model it — an electric (vapour-compression) chiller is a HeatPump in cooling mode, and the reference plant HeatingHeatPumpCoolingPlant holds one as compression_chiller — but swapping the module is a plant change, not a parameter change: simulate only takes changes to an existing design, and I have no plant-building tool. I asked the design lead's team to build the variant and the request came back unroutable: no member of that team can build a plant either.

I also tried to get at least a one-point COP for the compression chiller with evaluate_module (HeatPump, cooling mode, 10 MW, carnot fraction 0.5, 10 K minimum lift, 20 degC setpoint, chilled water 25 → 20 degC). The call returned a non-physical negative temperature lift, so I am not reporting a COP from it — it is not a valid operating point.

What the comparison would need, and what I would hand back once it exists: a plant build of design/v1 with chiller replaced by a HeatPump in cooling mode of the same 10 MW, grid.power_out → chiller.power_in, cool_users.return_out → chiller.source_in, chiller.sink_out → chiller_cooler.fluid_in; then a full-year simulate on case hackathon_2026 and a compare against result/b028bd65-8c35-4e44-ae84-2ad067938ccc on cold delivered, electricity bought, CAPEX, LCOC and CO2. The absorption baseline to beat is: cold delivered 23 743.0 MWh, electricity bought 26 728.0 MWh, CAPEX 34.636 MEUR, LCOC 51.445 EUR/MWh, CO2 13.774 kt (result/b028bd65-8c35-4e44-ae84-2ad067938ccc, compute_kpis; costs from the economist on the case's economics, EUR at 2015 prices, discount rate 0.05, 25 yr, heat-cost share 0.8).

The absorption choice rests on the assumption that the 100 degC line is available whenever cooling is needed — true in this design because the heat pump and boiler run year-round — and on the case's 300 g/kWh grid intensity; a much cleaner grid would weaken the case for absorption.
